Precipitation adds depth — rain in spring, summer and autumn; slow drifting snow in winter, with snow on the ground. Wind sways plants and roughens the water. Heat evaporates depth and dries animals faster. At 0 °C or below the surface freezes — visual only, the solver keeps running. One full day/night cycle is 1 real hour; at night creatures move slower and rest sooner.

Model notes

Sandbox build of the Living River Lab. The river uses a 2D finite-volume conservative update for depth and horizontal momentum with local Lax–Friedrichs/Rusanov numerical fluxes, bed-slope source terms, rainfall, friction, evaporation, and wet/dry clipping. The visible water surface is a 3D mesh. Map size, terrain shape, weather, season and time of day are live controls; resizing rebuilds the whole world. Creatures in five families share the watershed — the roster grows with the map size — each with its own randomized traits; the land adults build dams that act through the solver. Creature bones are articulated segment meshes driven by a force-based body controller and gait/swim joint targets.

Scientific limits

This is an executable visual research prototype, not a validated watershed model or species-specific biomechanics model. The solver uses simplified boundary conditions and a reduced 2D horizontal domain; vertical flow is represented visually by surface elevation. Identity and ablation scores are operational proxies, not established biological measurements. The ice state is visual only. Season and day/night effects are simplified visual and behavioral rules, not calibrated phenology or circadian models. Calibrate units, validate conservation and convergence, and compare against reference cases before scientific use.
